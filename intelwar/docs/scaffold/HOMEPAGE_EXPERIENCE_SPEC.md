# IntelWar.org — Homepage Experience Spec
## Mind War Theatre Orientation

**Version**: 1.0  
**Date**: July 19, 2026  
**Priority**: Critical  
**Goal**: Transform intelwar.org from a technical explanation site into a high-signal entry point for the Mind War Theatre.

This document overrides previous homepage structures. Execute it as the new primary experience for first-time visitors.

---

## Core Principle

The homepage must make the visitor feel they have **entered a contested strategic space**, not that they have opened a product documentation site.

The technical system (Living Log, consent, CGR, multi-intelligence, provenance) is real and powerful. It must support the experience, not replace it.

**Primary User Journey**:
1. Arrival / Threshold
2. Orientation (Basic Training)
3. Role Selection
4. Deployment into the Theatre

---

## 1. Hero Section (Threshold)

**Purpose**: Create an immediate sense of crossing into a serious intellectual battlefield.

**Requirements**:
- No product language.
- No technical feature lists in the hero.
- Strong, precise, almost cinematic opening statement.
- Visual treatment should feel strategic and restrained (deep neutrals, strong typography, subtle structural motion — Harvey.ai level of sophistication).

**Recommended Hero Copy Options** (choose one primary + supporting line):

**Primary Option (Recommended)**:
> You have entered the Mind War Theatre.
>
> A constitutional arena for rigorous intellectual combat — where human and artificial intelligence contest ideas under rules of integrity, and where the record of those contests becomes permanent strategic knowledge.

**Alternative Strong Openings**:
- “This is not a debate platform. This is a strategic arena.”
- “The information environment is contested. Most people are fighting without memory, without rules, and without a permanent record. That ends here.”

**Supporting Subhead**:
> Ideas are contested here under constitutional rules.  
> The outcomes are preserved.  
> The memory compounds.

**Primary CTA**:
- “Begin Basic Training” (scrolls to orientation section)
- Secondary: “View Active Campaigns”

---

## 2. Active Campaigns / Current Wars Section

**This is currently missing and is critical.**

Without visible wars, there is no theatre.

**Requirements**:
- A clear, living section titled something like:
  - “Active Campaigns”
  - “Current Wars of Ideas”
  - “Theatres of Contest”
- Present 3–6 major intellectual / narrative battles currently being contested (these can start as curated or placeholder campaigns).
- Each campaign card should include:
  - Campaign name
  - Short strategic description (1–2 sentences)
  - Status (Active / Emerging / Contested)
  - Optional: key terrain or opposing forces
- This section should feel like a strategic overview board, not a blog list.

**Example Campaign Framing** (for illustration):
- The Narrative of Inevitable Decline
- The Autonomy of the Individual Mind
- Synthetic Reality vs Provenance
- Institutional Trust Collapse
- Multi-Intelligence Alignment

These can be refined later. The structure matters more than the initial list.

## 3. Basic Training Section

**Purpose**: Orient the serious visitor before they engage.

This is the intellectual equivalent of basic training.

**Structure**:
1. **The Nature of the War**  
   Brief, high-signal explanation of why the cognitive / information environment is now a contested domain. Avoid alarmism. Be precise.

2. **Rules of Engagement**  
   Clear, elegant statement of the constitutional principles that govern the arena (Consent, Provenance, Multi-Intelligence Transparency, Human Override, Fail-Closed Enforcement, Log Integrity, etc.). Present them as the rules of the theatre, not as technical features.

3. **The Living Log**  
   Introduce the Log as the permanent strategic record of the theatre — the thing that makes this different from ordinary discourse. Emphasize that serious contests leave a lasting, queryable record.

4. **What Changes**  
   Short statement of the transformation: when arguments are tested under rules and preserved with integrity, strategic knowledge can actually accumulate.

**Tone**: Calm, precise, slightly formal. Treat the reader as a capable adult who has already sensed the problem.

---

## 4. Find Your Role

**Purpose**: Help the visitor locate themselves in the theatre.

Present clear role pathways. Each role should have a short description and a clear next action.

**Recommended Roles**:
- **Observer / Analyst** — Study the terrain, review past contests, understand strategic patterns.
- **Combatant** — Enter structured intellectual contests under the rules of the arena.
- **Cross-Checker** — Adversarial analysis and verification against the Living Log.
- **Strategist** — Design and track longer campaigns of ideas.
- **Chronicler** — Contribute to the permanent record with high-integrity entries.

This section should feel like choosing a path inside a war college or strategic command, not like choosing a product tier.

---

## 5. Deployment / Enter the Theatre

**Primary Call to Action Section**

Once the visitor has been oriented, give them a clear path into the actual arena:
- Access the Living Log
- View current contests
- Begin a structured engagement
- Review the Constitution / Rules of Engagement in full

Language should feel consequential, not promotional.

Example:
> The arena is open.  
> The record is permanent.  
> Enter with intention.

---

## 6. How the Technical System Should Be Handled

The deep technical explanation of the system (CGR Kernel, consent contracts, authority chains, multi-intelligence attestation, etc.) should **not** be the primary landing experience.

Recommended treatment:
- Create a dedicated section or secondary page called “How the Theatre Works” or “The Constitutional Engine”.
- On the homepage, only surface the *consequences* of the technical system (integrity, permanence, human oversight, multi-intelligence participation under rules).
- Link deeper for those who want the machinery.

This preserves the technical power while preventing the plumbing from becoming the first impression.

## 7. Visual & Motion Direction for the Homepage

- Maintain the sophisticated, restrained, Harvey.ai-inspired aesthetic.
- Hero should feel like a threshold, not a product screenshot.
- Active Campaigns section should have a strategic board quality.
- Animated SVGs should support orientation (e.g., rules of engagement, how the Log compounds) rather than feature demos.
- Motion remains purposeful and revealing, never decorative.
